# Art direction reference — human-centered, differentiated UI

## Establish a concrete visual thesis
Example, only as inspiration:
- Grocery: vibrant ingredient photography, generous natural whitespace, prominent search/departments, honest transparent prices, seasonal produce textures. Do not add fake organic certifications.
- Industrial software: confident dense information with legible operational statuses, restrained color, useful tables and filters; prioritize workflows over marketing hero.
- Boutique hospitality: immersive location photography and editorial typography; demonstrate booking actions only when wired.
- Professional services: crisp, credible typographic rhythm; avoid invented client logos or testimonials.

## Composition mechanics
- Lead the landing page with the actual task/value. Hero can be asymmetric and product-focused. Avoid default centered gradient + three identical cards unless brief makes it right.
- Use a typography scale and limit line lengths. Do not use tiny uppercase eyebrow over every heading.
- Responsive layout is designed, not simply stacked; decide what to hide, move, resize or prioritize at 320 px.
- Contrast/focus/44px touch targets; honor prefers-reduced-motion. A11y is a base requirement.
- Use credible concise copy: explicit action verbs, no dead CTAs, no fabricated claims.

## Screenshot critique rubric (0–5 each)
1. Brand distinctiveness / relation to industry.
2. Hero/product photography relevance and crop (or meaningful data hierarchy for dashboards).
3. Typography/hierarchy and information scanning.
4. Component consistency/spacing and visual restraint.
5. Mobile, RTL, dark and accessible states.

Target >= 20/25; any <=2 needs a fix. Score honestly with visible proof, not imagined screenshots. An objectively broken interaction fails regardless of visual score.
